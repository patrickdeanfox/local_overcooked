## Dynamics
- **Rats.** "Rats come out of holes in the countertops and steal the tomatoes, onions and mushrooms, regardless of if they're chopped or not. They will go back to where they came from and the only way to hold them back is to sprint into them."
- So: a rat spawns at a hole, walks to the nearest unattended ingredient on a counter or on the floor, picks it up and carries it back into its hole, where it is gone. Dashing into a rat stops it — the [Dash](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Dash) page's only combat use in the game.
- No spawn interval, walk speed, carry rule or hole count appears anywhere on the wiki. All `(estimate)`: four holes, one rat per hole, a rat every 10 s per hole while any ingredient is unattended, rat speed about 3 tiles/s.
- The serving conveyor is the second moving element: the hatch is fed by a belt rather than reached directly. Speed and direction are `(estimate)`.

## Cooperation notes
- All three wiki strategies are about the rats: do not leave ingredients unsupervised; put ingredients straight into pots rather than parking them on counters; and "if you have more than two players, have one be dedicated to holding off the rats".
- That last line is the design. At two players there is no spare chef, so the level is really a discipline test: the ordinary Overcooked habit of buffering chopped parts on a counter is exactly what loses you the run.
- The bottleneck is therefore counter space you cannot use. Six pots is a lot of cooking capacity; the constraint is that nothing can wait anywhere between the board and the pot.

## Recreate checklist
- Rats: a wandering NPC that targets items resting on counters, removes them, and can be stopped by a dash. The clone has pedestrians, which walk a lane and shove; a rat needs to path to an item and take it.
- A dash with a knock-back that affects NPCs.
- Conveyor belts, for the serving conveyor.
- Nothing else — three soups, pots, sink, boards, crates and bins all exist.
