# oc1 4-4 — Munch Mansion (mirrored halves, conveyor to a bin)

Source: https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/4-4_(Overcooked!) · Screenshots: `docs/research/screens/oc1/4-4.jpg`

## Facts
- Time limit 4:00. Timer starts immediately. Unlock: **42 stars**
  ([Stars to Unlock](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Stars_to_Unlock)); the infobox says 26.
  Player counts 1-4. 4-star difficulty "Hard".
- Recipes: **two families at once** — Burger, Lettuce Burger, Salad Burger, Pizza, Pepperoni Pizza,
  Mushroom Pizza. 6 order variants, the most of any world-4 level. Cadence not published: initial 2,
  one new order every 22 s, max 4 on screen, 75 s each `(estimate)`. The Strategies note "there are
  generally more Pizza Orders", so the draw is weighted toward pizza `(estimate: 60/40)`.
- Stars. **OC1**: 3★ only — 100 (1P), 120 (2P), from the
  [Overcooked! Levels](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Overcooked!_Levels) overview table.
  **AYCE**: 80/180/280/460 (1P), 80/200/320/520 (2P), 140/320/520/800 (3P and 4P).
  **AYCE Switch**: 60/120/200/320 (1P), 60/160/260/420 (2P), 120/260/420/660 (3P),
  100/220/340/540 (4P).
- Plates: sink kitchen, **one sink per half**. 4 plates in total, 2 per half — the left pair sits on
  the south wall, the right pair on the dividing run. Each half has its own plate return, so dirty
  plates come back on the side that served them.

**Correction to `docs/research/oc1-levels.md`.** That file's layout note says "Left half cooks
pizza (ovens, pizza crates); right half cooks burgers (pans, burger crates)". The wiki says the
opposite and the screenshot agrees: **the left half has the pans and the pizza crates, the right
half has the ovens and the burger crates.** That inversion is the whole point of the level — each
half owns the cookware for the *other* half's ingredients.

## Layout
- Grid size: **19 x 12**, counted from `4-4.jpg`; confidence **med**. Method: the four ingredient
  boxes along the south-west run measure 50 px each and the four along the right dividing run 45 px,
  which sizes each half at 9 columns; the central conveyor is one column; rows come from the
  north-wall-to-south-wall span at ~37 px per row. The two halves are mirror images about the
  conveyor, which cross-checks the column count.

- Legend extensions used: `N` oven, `v` conveyor belt running **down** (south) the centre column,
  `H` cheese crate, `&` dough crate, `!` pepperoni crate.
- Station inventory: boards 2 (one per half), pot burners 0, **pan burners 2** (left half),
  **ovens 2** (right half), fryers 0, mixers 0, steamers 0, sinks 2 (one per half), serve counters 2
  (2 tiles each), plate returns 2, bins 1 (at the foot of the conveyor), extinguishers 1 (left half;
  none is visible on the right in this capture), plates 4, conveyor tiles 10, crates 8 —
  left: pepperoni, mushroom, dough, cheese; right: beef, bun, lettuce, tomato. Plain counters 45.
  Floor 143, counter 85, station 40.
- Spawns: the capture puts one chef in the upper-right area at (11,3) and one in the lower-left at
  (5,8) `(estimate)`.
- Zones — **four sealed areas** plus the belt:
  - **Upper-left** (cols 1-8, rows 1-5): sink; reaches the pans and board on the dividing run.
  - **Lower-left** (cols 1-8, rows 7-10): plate return, serving counter, the four pizza crates and
    two plates on the south wall.
  - **Upper-right** (cols 10-17, rows 1-5): two ovens on the north wall, a chopping board beside the
    belt; reaches the burger crates and two plates on the dividing run.
  - **Lower-right** (cols 10-17, rows 7-10): sink, plate return, serving counter.
  - Left ↔ right crosses **only by the conveyor** (`v`), which runs south into the bin at (9,11):
    miss the catch and the item is destroyed. Upper ↔ lower within a half is a **height shift**,
    not a walk — the areas rise and fall, and only line up intermittently.
  - Longest crate-to-serve walk **inside one half** is 8 tiles; the cross-half trade cannot be
    walked at all, which is why the belt is the level.

## Dynamics
- **Conveyor belt** down the centre, terminating in a trash bin. Speed and belt length are not
  published; ~1.5 tiles/s over a 10-tile run, so ~7 s from the north end to the bin `(estimate)`.
  Per [Trash Bin](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Trash_Bin), cookware binned this way
  **respawns after 5 s** where it started; ingredients are simply lost.
- **Shifting floor heights.** Infobox: "Moving Countertops, Separated Chefs". Overview: "The kitchen
  constantly switches the height of the kitchen areas, allowing the other chef to share ingredients."
  Period and phase are not published — ~20 s per height cycle `(estimate)`.
- No falling hazard, no fire source other than burnt pans and ovens.

## Cooperation notes
- Wiki Strategies: "Chefs can prepare ingredients on their own chopping boards if they can't send
  them through the Conveyor Belt yet, while the other can"; and "Try to keep 3 Plates on the Pizza
  side, as there are generally more Pizza Orders" — so plates are meant to be traded across the belt
  too, not just ingredients.
- The bottleneck is the belt: it is one-directional (south) and ends in a bin, so every trade is a
  timed hand-off, and a missed catch costs both the ingredient and the prep that went into it.
- The intended division is one chef per recipe family, each cooking with the cookware they have and
  begging the other for the ingredients they lack.

## Recreate checklist
1. **Conveyor belts** — a tile that carries the item on it one tile per tick toward its facing, and a
   bin at the end. Nothing in the clone moves items.
2. **Oven station** plus the pizza recipe family (chop dough/cheese/tomato, assemble on the dough,
   bake, plate).
3. **Dough, cheese and pepperoni crates.**
4. **Shifting floor heights** that connect and disconnect two areas — the clone's `gate` dynamic is a
   thin approximation (walkable for `openSec` of every `periodSec`); a faithful version needs
   per-area elevation and throwing.
5. Two independent sinks / plate returns / serving counters in one level, and orders drawn from two
   recipe families with a weighted mix.
