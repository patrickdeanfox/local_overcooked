# oc1 2-3 — Treacle Town
Source: https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/2-3_(Overcooked!) · Screenshots: oc1/2-3.jpg

## Facts
- 4:00, no prep time. Unlock: the Stars to Unlock table says 15; the infobox says 9. The table is the better source. 1-4 players. The level that introduces Conveyor Belts.
- The three burgers, three variants. No cadence on the wiki; `orders` is `(estimate)` — initial 2, every 26 s, max 4, 110 s each, slow because every part of every burger has to ride the belt at least once.
- OC1 star columns blank. 3-star only, from the overview table: 1P 120, 2P 180. Provenance `oc1`; lower thresholds `null`.
- `plates = Yes`: sink on the west chef's side, plate return on the east chef's side, so a dirty plate has to travel the belt to be washed and travel back to be used. Three plates, from the screenshot; the wiki gives no count for this level (it does for 2-4).

## Layout
- Grid size: 13 x 8, counted from oc1/2-3.jpg; method: the south belt's tiles are unambiguous (56 px pitch, six clear tiles west of the central wall), and the wall is one tile at the image's vanishing x. The north run reads as seven at 43.4 px, which cannot be reconciled with six at the south; six was taken because the south seams are crisp and the north band's chevrons are easy to double-count. Station rows come from the two vertical runs beside the wall; confidence **med**
- The chevron-grey tiles are the conveyor belt, not a counter texture. Zoomed in they are rubber with roller feet under the front edge, and the chevrons on the south run all point west while the north run's point east — exactly the clockwise loop the Overview describes. The pale teal tiles are ordinary counters. Note the consequence: the belt's west and east columns run *behind* the crate and station runs, so they can only be boarded from the north and south rows. That is faithful to the screenshot but it is the part of this grid most worth re-checking.

- Zones:
- **West room** (columns 2-4 for rows 1-6, plus (5,3) and (5,5)): sink (1,1) and rack (1,2); beef, lettuce, bun and tomato crates at (1,3)-(1,6); two pans on burners at (5,2) and (5,4); the fire extinguisher at (5,1); a bin at (5,6).
- **East room** (columns 8-10 for rows 1-6, plus (7,3) and (7,5)): two chopping boards at (7,2) and (7,4); three plates at (8,1), (7,6) and (9,6); the serving counter at (11,3); the plate return at (11,4); a bin at (10,6).
- **The wall** (column 6): a brick pillar the full height of the room. It is scenery, not a counter — nothing can be put on it.
- **The belt** (the whole perimeter, rows 0 and 7 and columns 0 and 12): the only route between the rooms. It runs clockwise — east along the north row, south down the east column, west along the south row, north up the west column. Items are put on and taken off the north and south runs, which have floor beside them; the east and west columns are walled off behind the crate and station runs and are transit only.

## Dynamics
- One conveyor belt, running clockwise around the whole kitchen. "The Chefs must use it to send Ingredients and Plates." No speed on the wiki, so it is `(estimate)`; the Dash page's note that a dash "outruns conveyor belts" puts an upper bound on it — well under a chef's 4.2 tiles/s.
- A workable model: 1.5 tiles/s `(estimate)`, so a full lap of this 36-tile perimeter takes about 24 s and a west-to-east delivery about 8-12 s depending on where it is boarded.
- Nothing else moves. `dynamic = No` on the infobox refers to the level's floor, not the belt.

## Cooperation notes
- The Overview names the split precisely: west has ingredient boxes, two pans on burners, the sink and the fire extinguisher; east has the chopping stations, the serving counter, the plate return and the plates.
- That is a deliberately awkward split. The west chef has the raw beef but no board; the east chef has the board but no pan. So a beef patty crosses the belt twice — raw to the east to be chopped, chopped back to the west to be fried, fried east again to be plated. Bun and lettuce and tomato cross once or twice each.
- The plates make it worse in the same direction: they live east, they are washed west. Batching is the whole skill — load the belt with several items at once rather than sending them singly, because latency, not throughput, is the cost.
- The level has no strategy section on the wiki. Everything above is read off the station split.

## Recreate checklist
- Conveyor belts: a counter-height tile that carries whatever is placed on it at a fixed speed and hands off to the next belt tile, and a chef who can board or unload from an adjacent floor tile.
- A belt that runs as a loop through corners.
- Nothing else — burgers, pans, boards, sink, plate return, crates and bins all exist in the clone.
