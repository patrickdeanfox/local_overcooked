# Overcooked! 1 — main campaign levels

Source: the Overcooked wiki, fetched 2026-09-08. Every level's own page is linked in the
table. Screenshots are in `docs/research/screens/oc1/`.

## Reading the star columns

The wiki's per-level Star Chart has three blocks of columns: **OC1**, **AYCE**, and **AYCE
Switch**. The OC1 block is filled in for only a handful of levels; for the rest the editors
left it blank and filled the remaster columns instead.

- **`OC1` rows are the real Overcooked! 1 thresholds.** Full 1★/2★/3★ triples come from the
  level Star Charts (Intro Apocalypse, 1-1, 1-2) and from the
  [Level 1](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Level_1) summary page (1-3 … 1-6, 1P only).
  3★-only values for 1-1 … 4-4 come from the
  [Overcooked! Levels](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/Overcooked!_Levels) overview table.
- **`AYCE-SW` rows are marked and are *not* OC1 values.** They are the *All You Can Eat*
  Nintendo Switch thresholds, used only where OC1 has nothing. They are systematically
  higher than OC1's, because AYCE adds a 4th star and rescales. **Do not ship them as OC1
  numbers without halving-ish sanity checks.**
- Where the OC1 overview table and a level page disagree, I list the level page value; the
  only real conflict is 1-1's 2P 3★ (level Star Chart says 60, the overview table says 80).

Screenshot provenance: `1-1`, `1-2`, `1-3`, `1-4` and `5-2` are genuine OC1 captures.
**Every other `<level>.png` is an *All You Can Eat* remaster capture** — the layouts are
faithful, the art is not. Where both exist the AYCE one is suffixed `-ayce`.

## Layout notes

Each note is a paraphrase of the level's wiki Overview section. Notes for 1-1, 1-2 and 1-3
were checked against the OC1 screenshots; the rest are wiki-only.

### Intro Apocalypse
A single wide strip. Two chopping boards sit left of centre; a long counter runs to their
right and another to their left, with a one-tile gap in the left counter in the 1-player
layout. Lettuce and tomato crates are on the far side, and the **Ever Peckish itself is the
serving counter**. No sink, no pots. In 2-4 player the gap in the right counter is filled
in, which **fully separates the chefs** — one side chops, the other plates and serves, and
everything crosses over the counter. That counter hand-off is the whole lesson of the level.

### 1-1 — the one this clone starts from
A single rectangular room with one counter island floating in the middle of the floor.
Clockwise from the top-left: **onion crate** on the top wall; **burner with a pot** on the
top wall to the right of it; **sink** on the right wall; **serving counter and plate return**
in the lower-right; **two chopping boards** along the bottom wall; **two clean plates** on
the bottom counter near the boards. A **fire extinguisher** sits on the right wall above the
sink. The island counter is the only obstacle — it forces a detour between the onion crate
and the boards, so the intended two-player play is *pass onions over the island* rather
than walk around it. One pot is the real bottleneck: you must pour every finished soup onto
a plate before the next one can cook, which forces the sink into the loop.
*Wiki Strategies confirm all three: pass onions over the middle counters, pour out soups to
free the pot, and pre-chop during the prep phase because the clock is stopped.*

### 1-2
Two halves split by a **pedestrian crosswalk** running top-to-bottom through the middle.
**Right half:** onion and tomato crates on the lower counter, serving counter and plate
return on the right, trash bin at the top. **Left half:** sink at the left, two pots on
burners along the top, two chopping boards along the bottom. A counter runs alongside the
crossing so ingredients can be handed across without stepping into traffic. Crucially the
**bottom of the map is open** — the wiki notes you can walk right around underneath the
crossing instead of crossing it, which turns the pedestrians from a wall into a tax.
Two plates.

### 1-3
A ship deck, split left/right by a vertical run of counters that **slide back and forth as
the ship sways**. **Left:** two pots on burners at the top, three clean plates on the left
counter, trash bin and two chopping boards at the bottom. **Right:** serving counter and
plate stack at the top (no sink in this kitchen — plates come back clean), fire
extinguisher on the right, and the three ingredient crates (onion, tomato, mushroom) along
the bottom. Two open cargo grates in the middle of the deck block movement. As in 1-2 the
bottom edge is walkable, so the sliding counters gate hand-offs rather than sealing the
halves. The wiki also notes the **serving counter can be used from the back side**.

### 1-4
Two sectors joined by a single **1×3 corridor**. Right sector: five clean plates (the most
of any level in the series), plate return, serving counter, four pans on burners, spare
counters. Left sector: ingredient crates, sink, trash bin, chopping stations. Chopped beef
is meant to be parked on the middle counters for the other chef to fry.

### 1-5
An **oval one-tile-wide corridor** with every station on its rim. Ingredient crates
upper-left, two chopping boards lower-left (one in OC1 single-player), three pots on
burners lower-right, sink upper-right. The corridor is only wide enough for one chef, so
the intended play is both chefs circling in the same direction — take, chop, pot, serve —
never turning back. The alternative the wiki suggests is carrying pots to the boards to cut
the walk.

### 1-6
The only OC1 kitchen with an **elevated half**. A constant earthquake raises and lowers the
right side, so the two halves are connected only intermittently and chefs can drop down.
Left (low) side: tomato, lettuce and beef crates, chopping stations, sink, one pan, trash
bin. Right (high) side: three plates, three pans, serving counter, bun crate, plate return.
Everything gets **thrown** across at the moment the floors line up.

### 2-1
Two trucks on a highway. They drift apart and back together; when they are close you can
walk between them, otherwise you throw. **Lower truck:** two chopping stations, ingredient
crates, trash bin, fire extinguisher. **Upper truck:** bun crate, three plates, serving
counter, plate return, three pans on burners. No sink. Falling off costs 5 seconds.

### 2-2
Three sections plus a serving conveyor, each section joined to the next by a long strip of
floor. **Rats** emerge from holes in the counters, take tomatoes, onions and mushrooms
(chopped or not), and carry them back into the holes; the only counter is to sprint into
them. The level punishes leaving anything on a counter.

### 2-3
The chefs are fully separated and a **conveyor belt runs clockwise around the whole
kitchen**; it is the only way to move items between them. Left chef: ingredient crates, two
pans on burners, sink, fire extinguisher. Right chef: chopping stations, serving counter,
plate return, plates.

### 2-4
Same shape as 2-3 with two changes: three pans instead of two, and the **conveyors run into
trash bins** instead of looping. Anything you send and nobody catches is destroyed (and
cookware respawns 5 s later where it started).

### 3-1
An ice floe with all counters bunched in the middle and the floor **slippery**. South: deep
fryers and plates. West: sink. North: chopping stations and the fire extinguisher. East:
ingredient crates and one more deep fryer, with a trash bin in the upper-right. The serving
counter and plate return sit north, **detached from the rest of the counters**, so every
serve is a separate skid across the ice.

### 3-2
Chefs are fully separated. One side handles ingredients, cooking and serving; the other
chops and fetches plates from the plate return. The two roles must be swapped over the
course of the level, so the shared counters run hot in both directions. No sink.
The wiki's tip here is the general OC1 trick: **take the pot off the burner, and scoop soup
out with a plate.**

### 3-3
Trucks again, and the first kitchen with **two recipe families at once**. The supply truck
carries the plate return, serving counter and ingredient crates. The cooking trucks carry
pots on the left and deep fryers on the right. The wiki's advice — one chef per recipe
family — is the level's design: soup and fish burn on independent clocks.

### 3-4
A camp beside a frozen river with **ice floes drifting past as moving platforms**. Left
bank: all ingredient crates, two deep fryers with baskets, fire extinguisher, sink at the
bottom. Right bank: two chopping boards, plate return, serving counter, two plates, and one
extra easy-reach deep fryer. Falling in costs 5 s. The snowy patches are **not** slippery,
so the friction changes mid-kitchen.

### 4-1
A haunted forest kitchen divided by a set of **counters that float around freely** rather
than sliding on one axis — two carry chopping stations, three carry plates. As they move
they alternate which chef can reach the sink and which can reach the serving counter. Upper
half: three ovens. Lower half: ingredient crates. A narrow hallway on the left is the only
fixed route between the halves.

### 4-2
**Darkness.** The layout is simple but invisible except in the cone of the chefs'
flashlights. Top-right: serving counter, plate return, plates. Left of them: one pot on a
burner, then a chopping board. Top-left: a second chopping board and the sink. Mid-left: a
second pot, with the onion crate below it. A two-wide counter island sits in the middle
with the trash bin on its top face and the tomato crate on its bottom face. Mushrooms are in
the lower-left. Raw ingredients glow in the dark and can be used as landmarks.
(`4-2-lit.png` is the same kitchen with the lights on.)

### 4-3
Four quadrants. Lower-left: ingredient crates. Upper-left: the level's **single** chopping
board plus the sink. Both right quadrants: one burner with a pan each. Three plates sit on
counters between them; the plate return and serving counter are upper-right. The gimmick is
that **the burners, the beef crate and the chopping board themselves relocate**, sealing and
opening passages as they go — the chefs are separated by furniture that moves.

### 4-4
Two mirrored halves, each with its own serving counter, plate return, sink, two plates and a
chopping board. Left half cooks pizza (ovens, pizza crates); right half cooks burgers (pans,
burger crates) — **each half has the other's ingredients**, so the only way to fill an order
is to trade. The trade route is a **conveyor running down the middle into a trash bin**;
miss the catch and the ingredient is gone. The floor heights shift, opening and closing the
hand-off window.

### 5-1
Two sealed cooking chambers, left and right, each with a pan, a pot, two burners, a
chopping board, a serving counter, a plate return and two plates. All the *shared* stations
— every ingredient crate, the sink, the trash bin — live in a **shuttle chamber that slides
between the two sides**, moved by buttons. The button on the side the chamber is currently
on is green and sends it away.

### 5-2
Four quadrants over lava, joined by four rock bridges of which **only three are up at any
time**. Lower-right: serving counter and plate return. Lower-left: sink and all ingredient
crates. Upper-left: fire extinguisher, a pot on a burner, one counter, a chopping board.
Upper-right: trash bin, counters, two plates, two pans on burners. A **cannon fires down the
middle** of the kitchen. Counter space is so tight the wiki recommends throwing the
extinguisher on the floor to free its counter.

### 5-3
Chefs separated top and bottom, with two **pressure plates**: standing on yours opens a
passage for the other chef. The upper chef's plate lets the lower chef reach the chopping
boards; the lower chef's plate lets the upper chef reach every crate except rice. Left side:
fire extinguisher and burners with cookware at the bottom. Top: plates, rice crate, serving
counter, plate return. No sink.

### 5-4
Two separate platforms, each with ingredient crates and a chopping board — rice and beef on
the left, chicken and tortillas on the right. Three counters and three burners (one with a
pan, one with a pot, one empty) **orbit both platforms in a figure-8**, and are the only way
to pass anything across. Anything left on a moving burner drifts behind the serving counter
where nobody can reach it, and burns. No sink. The wiki rates this the hardest level in the
game for 4 stars.

### 5-5
An upper and a lower half plus **two side chambers that shuttle between them on buttons**.
Upper half: ingredient crates, pots on burners, serving counter, sink, plate return, spare
counters. Lower half: two chopping boards, three plates, a lot of counter space. The
chambers each hold counters and a trash bin; the intended play is to keep one chamber full
of raw ingredients and one full of chopped, and swap.

### 5-6
Four walled rooms with **pressure-plate doors** and **conveyor belts** between them.
Upper-left: serving counter, plate return, trash bin, and the conveyor down to the
lower-left. Lower-left: two pots on burners, sink, fire extinguisher, onion crate, and the
plate that opens the upper passage. Lower-right: three plates, tomato and mushroom crates,
and the conveyor up to the upper-right. Upper-right: chopping boards only, plus the plate
that opens the lower door. The wiki's trick is to send a **whole pot** down the conveyor
rather than ingredients one at a time.

### 6-1
A ship with one large counter through the middle carrying every ingredient crate, two pots
and the fire extinguisher. Top and bottom sections each have two burners; the serving
counter, plate return and three plates are at the top. The hazard is a block of **two
chopping boards plus a counter that travels around the ship clockwise**; when it slides
vertically, two loose counters on the opposite side shift too, and that is the only moment
chefs can change sides. No sink.

### 6-2
The **only level in the series with genuinely different layouts per player count**.
*1-3 players:* two mirrored self-sufficient kitchens, each with a serving counter, plate
return, two plates, a chopping board, two crates, two pots on burners and a sink — left has
tomato, right has mushroom, both have onion. Conveyors exist but only lead to bins, so
trading is pointless. *4 players:* one chef per quadrant; lower-left and upper-right hold
all crates, one pot and a sink, while upper-left and lower-right hold the serving counters,
plate returns, boards and plates — now the clockwise conveyors are mandatory.

### 6-3
3-4 made harder. A frozen river with **two rows of ice floes running in opposite
directions** (left row up, right row down), of varying sizes and arriving fast. Left bank:
a mid counter carrying the fire extinguisher splits it — two pots on burners above, trash
bin and onion + mushroom crates below. Right bank: three plates at the bottom with the
tomato crate beside them, plate return and serving counter above, then the sink and two
chopping boards at the top. You can walk the outer ledges to avoid the floes entirely.

### 6-4
A central kitchen ringed by **four chambers that rotate 90° on button presses**; only two
opposite chambers are reachable at a time. The centre is cramped: four counters (a fifth
holds the extinguisher), two chopping boards, four burners (two pots, two pans), a sink and
a trash bin. Rotating pair 1 carries the serving counters — lettuce crate + 2 plates +
plate return + serving counter on one side, mushroom crate + the same on the other.
Rotating pair 2 carries only crates — tomato + onion opposite bun + beef. Three recipe
families at once. Note the OC1-only quirk: **dirty plates appear at only one plate return at
a time.**

### The Peckening
Two buildings facing each other with a **helicopter-borne platform ferrying between them**,
and four scripted phases of five orders each. Left building is E-shaped: two burners, two
pots, two pans, a trash bin in the middle, an oven, the fire extinguisher, and a deep fryer
with a basket at the bottom. Right building is a tight 3-shape with the oven and deep fryer
in matching places, the trash bin in the upper-right, and a chopping board plus the sink in
the middle. The **Ever Peckish is the serving counter**, with the plate return below it
holding **3 dirty plates at level start** — the only OC1 kitchen that starts dirty.
The ferry platform is reconfigured for each phase (H-shaped counters, then conveyors, then
split by boards, then three tables) and meteors set counters on fire throughout; a meteor
strike on the right oven at the start of phase 2 is scripted.
